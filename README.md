# Bounded Rationality and the Myth of Continuity from Effective Is Real to Reason or Arrogance


Zou, Zhi Kai

https://orcid.org/0009-0000-4279-1064

> This article critiques the physics community's belief that continuity-based mathematics faithfully represents reality. It argues that "effective is real" overestimates human rationality, that our cognitive and computational limits render all models coarse-grained extrapolations, and that no current formalism—including continuity—will escape the historical fate of being superseded by deeper understanding.

---



A portion of the physics community holds that infinite approximation and continuity are faithful depictions of actual reality, even though no object in the natural world has ever been found to perfectly embody such continuity. This view asserts that "what is effective is real"—which is, in essence, a form of a priori mathematical Platonism, a metaphysical stance. However, I believe this philosophical position significantly overestimates the scope of human rationality.

In my view, even mathematicians and physicists must candidly acknowledge that human rationality is fundamentally bounded. This entails recognizing that the mathematical systems we have developed based on continuity are inherently limited, and very likely do not constitute a faithful mapping of the real world. The advancement of human knowledge proceeds in stages; we must not mistake the current efficacy of a mathematical formalism for perpetual invincibility, nor should we abandon the pursuit of nature's ontological essence merely because a given model works well at present.

At its core, any philosophical position rooted in mathematical Platonism that relies on continuity models effectively treats the cognitive achievements of the current era as the terminus of knowledge—and this is a form of intellectual arrogance. Such arrogance is by no means uncommon in the history of human inquiry into nature. In every era, the dominant theory has been regarded as the ultimate reality, as the true representation of nature—only to be overturned by subsequent cognitive advances. There is no reason to believe that the current continuity-based mathematical models will be exempt from this pattern.

From another perspective, human civilization is merely an infinitesimal part of the universe. The computational power at our disposal—the upper bound of our rationality—is far below the complexity of the cosmos. Even if we could approach a form that mirrors the ontological essence of nature, it would at best be a coarse-grained homomorphic extrapolation. In other words, human cognition can never achieve a fully faithful representation of reality.
